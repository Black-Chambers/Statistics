# Works without 'E'

## Gadsby

"Gadsby" by Ernest Vincent Wright, published in 1939.
It's a full-length novel (around 50,000 words) that avoids using the letter "e" entirely - quite a feat in English, since "e" is the most common letter in the language.
Wright reportedly tied down the "E" key on his typewriter to keep himself from slipping up.

*Gadsby* is now in the public domain when the copyright was not renewed.
Available on Project Gutenburg as ebook and Internet Archive as scanned pages or ebook.

Fun trivia from the search: despite Wright's efforts, the published version actually contains four accidental instances of "e" - three in the word "the" and one in "officer", discovered by later readers who combed through it.
So even with the typewriter key physically disabled, a few slipped through in revisions or typesetting.

## A Void

**"La Disparition"** (1969) by French author Georges Perec, later translated into English as **"A Void"** by Gilbert Adair.<br>
The translation also avoids the letter "e"
